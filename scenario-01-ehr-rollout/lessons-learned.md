# Lessons Learned — EHR System Rollout

> **Note:** This is a fictional case study created to demonstrate technical 
> project management skills. It is not based on a real employer, client, 
> or project.

## Purpose
Captured at project closeout to document what worked, what didn't, and 
what would be done differently on a future rollout of this kind.

## What Went Well

- **Pilot-first approach reduced risk significantly.** Issues that would 
  have been costly at scale (workflow gaps, billing reconciliation errors) 
  were caught and fixed at one site before affecting the other five.
- **Train-the-trainer model paid off.** Having super users on the floor 
  during go-live reduced dependency on IT and vendor support for routine 
  questions, and staff were more comfortable asking peers than submitting 
  formal tickets.
- **Weekly steering committee cadence kept leadership aligned.** No major 
  decisions stalled waiting on sponsor approval, since issues were surfaced 
  and resolved in the same week they came up.
- **Phased/staggered rollout protected IT bandwidth.** Spacing go-lives 
  2–3 weeks apart meant the IT team was never supporting more than one 
  site's hypercare period at a time.

## What Didn't Go Well / Challenges

- **Initial training schedule underestimated billing/front-desk complexity.** 
  These roles were given shorter training windows than clinical staff, which 
  led to billing workflow confusion at the pilot site. Training time for 
  these roles was extended for all subsequent sites.
- **Staff resistance was underestimated early on.** Change management 
  communication started later than it should have — earlier and more 
  frequent messaging about "why this change" (not just "how to use it") 
  would have reduced pushback.
- **Vendor configuration timelines slipped slightly at 2 of the 6 sites**, 
  due to unclear specialty-specific requirements gathered too late in the 
  process. Requirements gathering should have started
